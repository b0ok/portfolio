How I built an AI receptionist for my barbershop

Terry "T3" Nguyen · October 2026 · 4 min read

I cut hair from noon to eight. When I'm with a client, I'm not answering my phone. But the texts don't stop: how much is a fade? Are you open Sunday? How do I book? Same questions, every day. Every one I miss is a client who might book somewhere else.

So I built something to answer them for me.

What it does

There's a chat bubble on t3trims.netlify.app. Clients tap it and ask whatever they'd normally text me. It answers like a front desk would:

  • Prices: Fresh Up, VIP, and the mobile "Barber on the GO!" service
  • Promos like the student cut and every 10th cut free
  • Hours, location, and how to reach me after hours
  • A link to book through my Square page
  • Not sure what cut to get? It sends them to my Style Guide

How it works, in plain English

When someone sends a message, it goes to a small function running on Netlify, the same place my site is hosted. That function sends the question to an AI model (Meta's Llama, through NVIDIA's API) along with a fact sheet about my shop, then sends the answer back to the chat bubble.

The API key lives on the server, never in the website code, so nobody can grab it and run up my bill.

The fact sheet is the whole trick

An AI model left on its own will happily make things up. Ask it about a barbershop and it'll invent a price that sounds right. So the model only gets my real info, plus strict rules:

Only answer questions about T3Trims. If you don't know, say so and tell them to call or text the shop. Never make up information. You cannot create a booking yourself. Keep answers short, like a text message.

It even knows what not to say. I used to run a "$20 Tuesdays" deal. It's over, so the fact sheet tells the bot that if someone asks, the promo has ended. One less awkward conversation at the chair.

The catch: when I change a price, I have to update the fact sheet too. The bot is only as right as what I give it.

What broke

The first version ran on Supabase's free plan. Free projects there get paused when they don't get enough activity, and one day mine did. That took my receptionist offline. I moved the whole thing into a Netlify function next to my site, and it's been running there since.

Lesson: for something clients rely on, check what the free plan does when you're not looking.

If you run a small business

  • Start with your real FAQ. Write down the ten questions you answer most. That's your fact sheet.
  • Tell it to say "I don't know". A wrong answer about a price costs you more than no answer.
  • Keep a human route. Mine always points people to call or text me.
  • Don't let it take actions yet. Mine sends people to the booking page. It never books, cancels or charges anyone itself.
